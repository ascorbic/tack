The search is anything GitHub's issue search understands, such as `repo:owner/name is:open label:bug`. Under the field, **How Tack reads it** shows its parts as chips: where it looks, what it filters, the words it searches for, and its order. A `sort:` in the search wins over **Sort by**, which then says so and is turned off.

The panel also says what refreshes it: "Live: refreshes when owner/name changes." for a search in one repository, or "Searches everywhere you can see: refreshes every few minutes." for one with no repository. See [when widgets refresh](/widgets/overview/#when-widgets-refresh).

## Using it

Each row shows the item's state, title, repository and number, up to three labels and two assignees. Rows already on the board have a pin (click it to find the card); the rest link to GitHub. The header shows the search as chips; point at them (or Tab to them) to read the whole search, with **Copy** and, for GitHub, **Open on GitHub**. The footer counts the results, "30 of 112". A list that searches for words, or everywhere, and finds nothing says "Nothing matches this search" and offers to edit it, since waiting won't help.

- **Drag a row out** onto the board to add a card for it, or **click it** (or Tab to it and press Enter) to add the card in free space beside the list. The list doesn't change. A row whose card is already on the board shows that card instead.
- **A new list** asks "What should this list show?", with the [ready-made searches](/widgets/overview/#adding-a-widget) and **Write a search**.

## Drop to match

Drop cards onto a list, and Tack works out what would make them match its search, and offers to stage it: "Make #412 match", "So it shows in “Open bugs”. Staged, not pushed." The cards glide back to where they were.

It can stage labels added or removed, assignees, a milestone, and closing or reopening (with `reason:` deciding how it's closed). Some searches can't be matched by changing an item, and the list says why: words in the title, a different repository or author, whether a pull request is merged, or a repository without the app ("Tack can't change items in owner/repo: the app isn't installed there"). A list that searches for words says so in its settings: "dropping cards on it can't make them match".

The changes are staged like any other, and go to GitHub when someone [pushes](/writing-back/pushing/).
