This tour assumes an instance is running and you can sign in to it. If you're setting one up, start with [Deploy Tack](/self-hosting/deploy/).

## Make a board

<Steps>

1. Open your instance and choose **Continue with GitHub**. Tack uses your GitHub account: "You'll see what GitHub lets you see, and nothing changes on GitHub until you push it."

2. On the home page, choose **New board** (or **Create your first board**). It's called "Untitled board", with the title selected, so just type a name and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.

3. Paste an issue or pull request link onto the board with <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>V</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>V</kbd> on Windows and Linux). It becomes a live card. You can paste up to 50 links at once, one per line, and they're laid out in a grid. Or press <kbd>C</kbd> and type `owner/repo#123`.

</Steps>

## Sketch

<Steps>

1. Press <kbd>S</kbd> and click to add a sticky, then type. Each sticky shows who wrote it. Press <kbd>T</kbd> for text, for headings and notes.

2. Press <kbd>F</kbd> and drag out a **section** around some cards and stickies. Name it. Anything dropped inside moves with it.

3. Press <kbd>X</kbd> and drag from one shape to another to connect them. Or hover a shape with the select tool and drag one of the dots beside it.

4. Everyone else on the board sees all this live, with their cursors and names. Press <kbd>/</kbd> to chat beside your cursor, and click someone's avatar to follow their view.

</Steps>

## Stage and push

<Steps>

1. Select two stickies (drag a box around them, or <kbd>Shift</kbd>-click). A bar appears under the selection. Choose **Make 2 issues** (<kbd>⌥</kbd> <kbd>I</kbd>), pick the repository, and choose **Stage 2 issues**. A ghost card behind each sticky shows it's staged. Nothing has been written to GitHub.

2. Select a card and press <kbd>⌥</kbd> <kbd>L</kbd> to stage a label. Staged changes show on the card, and in the **Changes** button in the top bar.

3. Choose **Changes**, then **Review and push**. The push sheet shows every change with a preview: issue bodies, labels, and how many people will be notified.

4. Keep **Push as: You** and choose **Push 3 changes**. Each change lands one at a time, and the sheet says "All 3 changes are on GitHub" when GitHub confirms them. The stickies turn into their new issues' cards, in place.

</Steps>

## Share it

Choose **Share** in the top bar, and invite people by GitHub username, as **Can edit** or **Can view**. Or open the board to everyone on your instance, or to anyone with the link. See [Boards and sharing](/boards/boards-and-sharing/).
